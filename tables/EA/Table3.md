Table 3. Propositions, Their Class, and Evidence That Would Count Against Them

| Proposition | Class | Manipulated or observed variables | Outcome | Expected pattern | Result inconsistent with the proposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Path invariance | Normative | Acquisition pathway, holding directly evidenced attribute constant | Evaluation | No pathway effect unless a stated warrant applies | (Normative: assessed by argument; P1 is violated in practice where pathway effects persist without a stated warrant) |
| P2 Direct-evidence substitution | Empirical | Quality and coverage of D | Weight on and incremental information of B | Both decline as D improves, for B operating as a proxy | Weight on B stable or rising as D improves in quality and coverage of the same attribute |
| P3 Opportunity-to-credential pathway | Consequential | Independent weight on socially patterned B | Association between opportunity and selection outcomes | Association strengthens with credential weight | No change in the opportunity–outcome association when credential weight is added or removed, holding D constant |
| P4 Pathway substitutability | Normative | Degree to which pathways produce equivalent attribute | Strength of the case for invariance | Stronger case where pathways are substitutable | (Normative: assessed by argument) |
| P5 Proximal-evidence attenuation | Empirical | Availability of reliable proximal evidence | Incremental information of distal records | Smaller where proximal evidence is available | Distal records add as much or more information when reliable proximal evidence of the same attribute is present |
| P6 Measurement boundary | Empirical | Feasibility of sampling the target attribute | Reliance on biography | Greater reliance where the attribute is hard to sample | Reliance on biography unrelated to the feasibility of direct evidence across attributes or programs |

Note. B = biographical evidence; D = relatively direct evidence of the target attribute. Normative propositions state warrants and are assessed by argument; empirical and consequential propositions are testable with the designs described in the text.
