# SHE_FIT_AUDIT — Path-Invariant Selection

## 1. Why a higher-education paper rather than general selection theory?
The motivating problem — biography- and experience-sensitive selection — is a live, contested practice of universities (holistic review, contextual admissions, test-optional policies). Employment and politics appear only to show the argument's generality and its boundary. Roughly 75–80% of the manuscript is higher-education material.

## 2. Which SHE debates does it enter?
Holistic and contextualised admissions (Boliver; Gorard et al.), merit and its construction (Karabel; Stevens; Warikoo; Posselt), credentialism and stratification (Collins; Dore), inequality in participation and access (Snellman et al.; Hoxby & Avery; Chetty et al.), test-optional policy (Belasco et al.), cultural capital (Bourdieu; Lareau; Khan). All are standard SHE-reader territory.

## 3. Is university admissions clearly dominant?
Yes: sections 1–8 of the manuscript all centre admissions; Table 1 and Figures 1–3 foreground admissions; employment and politics each get one compact section.

## 4. Theoretically engaged rather than think piece?
It engages and differentiates from five traditions (human capital, signaling, cultural capital, credentialism, validity/fairness) and produces 6 testable propositions plus a falsifiable empirical agenda — beyond commentary.

## 5. Employment/politics sufficiently subordinate?
Employment: one section (~600 words) framed as structural generalisation, explicitly not a personnel-psychology review. Politics: one short section (~450 words), hypothetical candidates only, framed as the limiting case where direct observation is hardest. Combined < 20% of text; politics < 7%.

## 6. Genuinely new conceptual argument?
Yes — conditional criterion for residual biographical weight; see narrowest claim in NOVELTY_ASSESSMENT.md.

## 7. Avoids standardized-test advocacy?
Repeated explicit disclaimers: direct assessment ≠ paper testing; tests carry their own equity problems (Sackett et al. 2009 numbers reported both ways); the question is *institutional clarity about what is valued*, not a technology endorsement. Contextual admissions are explicitly protected.

## 8. International sense?
Cases span US holistic review, UK contextual admissions, examination-based systems (where biography weight is low and the question is reverse), and credential screens globally; framed as a general selection-architecture question.

## 9. Title aligned?
"When Should Experience Matter? Path-Invariant Selection and the Credentialisation of Experience in Higher Education" — interrogative, non-polemical, HE-flagged. Retained.

## 10. Desk-rejection risk reducers implemented
- First two pages open on the concrete HE problem, no equations.
- Abstract names HE debates and the contribution plainly.
- Claims calibrated (no "abolish holistic review"); international framing; citations concentrated in HE literature.
- Length held under the ~7,000-word total ceiling.
