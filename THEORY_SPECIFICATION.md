# THEORY_SPECIFICATION — Path-Invariant Selection

## Notation (used sparingly in text; defined here)
- R — resources and opportunities available to an applicant (family, school, institutional).
- E — experience (activities, roles, biographies actually undergone).
- P — acquisition pathway: the route through which an attribute was acquired.
- K — current relevant competence or attribute.
- D — direct assessment/observation of an attribute the institution values.
- B — biographical information (CV, essays, records of experience).
- Z — the set of attributes legitimately included in the institutional selection objective (explicitly a normative choice).
- A — selection evaluation/decision.
- Y — later outcome relevant to the institution (academic success, contribution, graduation — deliberately broad).

## Pathways (descriptive)
- Developmental: R → E → K → Y. Experience builds competence which produces outcomes.
- Credential: E → Credential signal → A. Possession of the experience is itself rewarded in evaluation.
- These coexist; the manuscript's object is when the second channel is justified.

## Experience value decomposition (descriptive)
V(E) = V_intrinsic + V_learning + V_credential. An experience can carry large intrinsic and learning value while properly carrying zero credential value.

## Path-Invariance Principle (normative)
A ⊥ P | Z: for acquisition pathways substitutable with respect to Z-relevant attributes, applicants demonstrating equivalent relevant attributes should not receive different evaluations merely because attributes were acquired via different pathways — *provided* the pathway carries no independently legitimate Z-relevant information. If the institution legitimately values an aspect of the pathway itself, that aspect belongs in Z by definition; the principle bites only on unjustified residual weight.

## Residual biographical value (descriptive scaffold, illustrative only)
RBV = I(Y ; B | D): the outcome-relevant information biography adds once valid direct evidence is known. Operational analogue R²(D + B) − R²(D). Explicitly: illustrative, not restricted to linear prediction, and prediction is not the only legitimate institutional objective.

## Experience–Equality Paradox (descriptive, conditional)
If S (social/institutional resources) → E, rewarding E as credential (S → E → A) can convert opportunity inequality into credential inequality, even where S → E → K (real learning) also holds. Broadening selection by recognising experience may reduce dependence on one unequal metric while opening further channels through which unequal opportunities acquire credential value. Stated conditionally; not a claim that holistic admissions necessarily increase inequality.

## Substitutability dimension (descriptive)
- High substitutability: different pathways plausibly produce equivalent K (academic knowledge, reasoning, language).
- Low substitutability: embodied, interactive, context-bound capability (clinical skill, ensemble performance, practical judgement). Even here, *undergoing* E differs from *being able to perform* K → supports performance assessment, not experience counting.

## Direct-assessment hypothesis (empirical)
Quality(D) ↑ → RBV ↓, for biography whose predictive relevance operates through the directly assessed construct — conditional on construct coverage, reliability, appropriate criterion, no omitted valued attributes, sufficient directness. Limiting thought experiment: if every presently valued attribute were perfectly observable, which past facts would still matter? (Conceptual; no claim that perfect measurement is feasible.)

## Propositions
- P1 (normative): Path invariance — for substitutable pathways, evaluation should become increasingly independent of pathway as direct evidence of legitimately valued attributes improves.
- P2 (empirical): Direct-assessment substitution — for proxy-biography, better direct measurement reduces residual biographical value.
- P3 (normative): Experience credentialisation — independent selection value attached to socially patterned experiences can create a pathway through which opportunity inequality is converted into credential inequality.
- P4 (normative): Substitutability — the stronger the substitutability, the stronger the case for path-invariant selection.
- P5 (empirical): Distal-credential attenuation — distal credentials contribute less incremental information when reliable proximal evidence is available.
- P6 (empirical): Measurement boundary — residual dependence on biography will be (and legitimately may be) greater where direct measurement of the relevant construct is less feasible.
- P7 (empirical): Strategic response — the larger the independent credential premium on an experience, the stronger the incentive to produce, accumulate, or present it strategically (Campbell/Goodhart dynamics).
